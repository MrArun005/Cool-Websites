Bolt Learning Live

Bolt is learning to get up after a fall, by trial and error, the way real robot dogs do it: first roll onto its belly, then stand. Every million practice steps the training saves a checkpoint. This page tests each one: it drops Bolt on its side, on its back, half tipped over and standing, and records what the brain does. How upright it gets

For each kind of fall: the most upright Bolt got during the attempt, at each checkpoint. 1 is standing, 0 is on its side, −1 is flat on its back. A filled dot means it reached the goal: standing (runs 17, 17b) or belly down and still (run 17c).

What changed and why

  1. Run 17 learned to lie still. It was charged for fast joint movement. Thrashing to roll over cost 1–5 points every step, far more than being upright paid, so the cheapest thing was to do nothing. Stopped at 2M steps.
  2. Can this body roll over at all? A search over motor sequences, with no learning, found a way to roll Bolt over from its side. So it is possible.
  3. Run 17b: no charge for fast joint movement, a fifth of the effort charge, a bonus every time Bolt gets more upright than it has been so far, and more random trying at the start.
  4. Run 17b learned to roll. By 2M steps it rolled from flat on its back onto its side, and from half tipped nearly level, but never stood up.
  5. How real robot dogs do it. ETH Zurich's ANYmal (Lee, Hwangbo and Hutter, 2019) recovers from any fall in under 5 s, over 97% of the time, with separate skills: self-righting (roll onto the belly, legs tucked), then standing up, then walking, picked by a selector. Unitree's recovery stand follows the same steps.
  6. Run 17c does the same. It continues from run 17b and only learns to roll onto its belly with its legs tucked and lie still. Bolt's posture brain already stands up from there: 20 of 20 from the lying pose, 17 of 20 with the legs well off it.
  7. Run 17c stalled at a tilt. It rolled past its side but always settled at the same 0.16 tilt. The brain could only move the sideways hip joints ±0.4 rad, and the joints stopped at ±0.5 rad. Real robot dogs swing their legs sideways about ±48°.
  8. Run 17d: a wider leg swing. Bolt's sideways hip joints now reach ±0.9 rad, and the self-righting brain can use each joint's whole range. One million steps later it rolled onto its belly 7 to 8 times in 10 from every kind of fall, in 1 to 1.7 s.
  9. The whole recovery. Self-right, fold the legs under the body (Unitree does this too), then the posture brain stands up: 15 to 17 falls in 20 end with Bolt standing, about 6 s after the fall.

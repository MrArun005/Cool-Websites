Where we are

Bolt has two trained brains that work together: one for standing, sitting and lying, one for walking, turning and running at any asked speed. Both stand and walk tall like a real dog, with lower legs close to upright and no knees on the floor. Saved in models/.

Stands 43 cm tall on four paws. Sits 30° nose-up on all four paws, both sides alike (the old brain sat lopsided with a paw in the air).

Walk, run and turn sharply

Goes the speed it is asked, from a slow walk to a run: 0.2, 0.4, 0.7, 1.0 and 1.2 m/s asked give 0.22, 0.41, 0.70, 0.99 and 1.18 m/s on bumpy, randomised physics, with no knees on the floor. Turns on command and takes over from the standing brain. Following a person, it ran at up to 1.19 m/s and never fell behind more than 1.9 m (the old walk brain fell 6 m behind).

Real dogs turn sharply by stepping sideways: front paws one way, rear paws the other. The trot now does that too. The 11.5M brain turns at the rate it is asked: up to 111°/s at a slow walk, 86°/s at 0.7 m/s, and 58°/s while running at 1.0 m/s, with no falls on bumpy, randomised physics, except 1 in 6 at the very sharpest (115°/s at 0.4 m/s). Running holds 0.96 and 1.15 m/s when asked 1.0 and 1.2, 0 falls in 8. Following people it stays 1.7 m behind (was 2.6 m), 0 falls in 80 simulated minutes.

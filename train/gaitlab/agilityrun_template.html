Bolt's Agility Run

You are the handler. Call Bolt round an agility course: walk, trot or run, jump the hurdles, sit or lie down on the pause table, and turn it where the line bends. Bolt moves with its real trained brains in real physics, running in your browser, so it behaves like a young dog in training: it needs a moment to change gait, it slows down to turn, and a fast landing can tip it over. When it falls it gets back up by itself, which costs time. Scoring is time plus faults: a knocked bar, a refusal or a missed obstacle each add 5 s. Beat the course time with no faults for a clear round.

      Handling tips, and where Bolt is weak

      • Turning: the dog-motion brain only turns gently, so for a sharp bend Bolt drops to a walk and another brain (run 12's walk) steps it round. Auto-steer follows the course line for you; the turn keys take over while you hold them.
      • Jumps: Bolt takes off about 1 m before the bar. Line up straight first: the jump brain only practised arriving within ~11° of square. 30 cm bars clear about 7 times in 10, 40 cm about 3 in 10.
      • Landings: Bolt lands at almost 4 m/s, faster than it can run, and the jump brain needs 5-8 m to stop it. It then sits for a moment to steady itself. About one landing in three still ends in a fall.
      • Sit and down: Bolt stops from a walk first. From a trot it shifts down through a pace (dropping straight to a walk fell 2 times in 8).
      • Run (canter) is fast but weak: the brain falls in about one canter in four.

      Brains from training runs 18b (dog motion, 17.5M steps), 16g (jump, 27M) and 17e (self-righting, 16.5M), plus the posture brain and run 12's walk for sharp turns. Real MuJoCo physics with the A1 motor model, the same as in training.

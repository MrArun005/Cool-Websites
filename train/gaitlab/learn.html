This page tests each one: it drops Bolt on its side, on its back, half tipped over and standing, and records what the brain does. For each kind of fall: the most upright Bolt got during the attempt, at each checkpoint. 1 is standing, 0 is on its side, −1 is flat on its back. A filled dot means it ended standing.

What changed and why

  1. Run 17 learned to lie still. It was charged for fast joint movement. Thrashing to roll over cost 1–5 points every step, far more than being upright paid, so the cheapest thing was to do nothing. Stopped at 2M steps.
  2. Can this body roll over at all? A search over motor sequences, with no learning, found a way to roll Bolt over from its side. So it is possible.
  3. Run 17b: no charge for fast joint movement, a fifth of the effort charge, a bonus every time Bolt gets more upright than it has been so far, and more random trying at the start.
  4. Next, if it stays stuck on its back: widen the sideways leg swing from ±29° to about ±50°, like real robot dogs. The motor search found a roll from the back only with the wider swing.

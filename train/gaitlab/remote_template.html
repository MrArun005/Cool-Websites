Bolt Remote

Bolt runs live in this browser: MuJoCo physics, and trained brains choosing every motor command fifty times a second. Drive it with the joystick, the keyboard or a game controller, or open this page on your phone and use that as the remote. Switch on Auto self mode and Bolt explores on its own, using only what its head-mounted scanner has seen: it maps the yard, steers round things, stops at the kerb, finds the hurdles and jumps them.

Drive Bolt

Keyboard: WASD or arrows (Shift faster), 1–7 skills, M Auto self mode, Space stop. Game controller: left stick drives; A jump, B sit, X lie down, Y stand, LB walk, RB trot, RT canter, Start Auto, Back stop. Walk, Trot and Canter keep Bolt going (steer with the stick) until Stop. Bolt cannot walk backwards: pulling back stops it. If no command arrives for half a second, Bolt stops.

How Auto self mode knows where things are

  • Its own scanner, nothing else. A depth scanner on Bolt's head casts 279 rays (31 across 120°, 9 rows from 2° up to 50° down), five sweeps a second, using MuJoCo's own ray caster against the physics. Each ray reports how far it went and what it hit: ground, road surface, something solid, or a hurdle, as a depth camera with a ground and object classifier would.
  • Its own map. From those rays Bolt builds a 25 cm grid of the yard: unknown until seen, then open ground, solid, road or hurdle. Where it goes, what it sniffs, where it naps and which hurdles it jumps all come from this map, never from the yard's plan.
  • Hurdles. Bolt works out each hurdle's position and line from the points it saw on it, and the bar's height from the bar itself (it walks up for a closer look if it has only seen the posts). The jump brain is handed that estimate, not the real bar.
  • Moving. It plans a path on its map (A*, unknown ground allowed but dearer), steers for the most open heading, and has two reflexes: never walk into something within half a metre, and stop when it sees road ahead.
  • What is simplified. Bolt's position comes straight from the simulator (a perfect odometry stand-in; a real robot's estimate drifts). The kerb has no height in this physics, so the road is a thin layer the scanner can tell apart. The scanner is levelled by the IMU and has no noise. Times between the phone and this screen are real.

Headless checks (train/gaitlab/remote_test.mjs, simulation, 29 Oct). Auto self mode, 8 runs of 4 minutes from an empty map: 18 of 18 jumps clean (bar up, all paws past the real bar, no fall), 0 times onto the road, 4 kerb stops, 3 bumps into something (10.6 s touching in 32 minutes), 7 falls, all 7 got up alone, 93% of the yard mapped on average; hurdles placed within 3 cm and bar heights within 1 cm of the real ones. Remote: with no command for 0.5 s Bolt stops (noticed after 0.52 s, standing 3.1 s later from a 1.8 m/s trot); 60 switches between Auto and manual, 0 falls; driven flat out at the house, the guard stopped it 1.15 m short of the porch; walked at the road, it stopped with the nose 0.58 m short of the kerb.

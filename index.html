Let's build a robot.

Every robot, from a toy to a factory arm, is the same five things: a body to hold it together, muscles (motors) to move, senses (sensors) to notice the world, a brain (a small computer with RAM and storage) to decide, and a battery to power it all. This page shows how each piece works, and walks you from a pile of parts to a robot that waves, drives around and remembers your name.

Robot lab: how limbs move

Each joint is a servo motor (the orange dots). A servo is a motor that turns to an exact angle from 0° to 180° and holds it. Drag the sliders and watch the commands the brain would send over the USB cable to the limb controller. These are the same commands the real firmware in this repo understands.

Memory, RAM and storage

    
Your robot's brain is a Raspberry Pi, a full computer the size of a credit card. Like any computer it has two kinds of memory. RAM is fast working memory for what the robot is thinking about right now, but it is wiped the moment the power goes off. Storage (the SD card or SSD) is slower but keeps everything forever. A robot that "remembers you" tomorrow has to save that to storage. Try it:

    

      
      
    

    

      

        
RAM: short-term memory

        Fast. Raspberry Pi 5 has 4–16 GB. Lost on power-off.
        
    
      
    
      
    
        
    Storage: long-term memory
    
        Slower. A 64 GB SD card or an SSD. Survives power-off.
        
      
      
      
    
      
    
      
      
      
    
      
    
      
    
      How much do you need? A talking, moving robot runs fine on 4 GB RAM. Get 8 GB if you want camera vision or a small AI model running on the robot itself. The little Arduino in the body has only 2 KB of RAM. That's plenty for moving motors, and it's why the thinking happens on the Pi.

        ⚠️ Safety rules
        
      
        Never power servos or motors from the Arduino's or Pi's 5 V pin, because they pull far more current and will reset or damage the board. Always connect all grounds (GND) together. Lithium batteries can catch fire if shorted or over-discharged, so use cells with a protection board, never leave them charging unattended, and add a power switch you can reach. Keep fingers clear of arm joints when testing; MG996R servos are strong.
        
    
        
  
        

        



        
  
        
    
        The code
        
    
        All of this is in the robot/ folder of this repo. You can try the brain on your laptop today, before you buy anything. It runs in simulation and prints what the motors would do.
        
    
        
      
        
        
        🧠 Brain (Raspberry Pi, Python)
        
        
        robot/brain/: brain.py understands commands, memory.py stores short-term memory in RAM and long-term memory in a SQLite file, and body.py talks to the Arduino.
        

        cd robot/brain
python3 brain.py              # simulated body
you> my name is Arun
robot> Nice to meet you, Arun!
you> wave
you> quit
python3 brain.py              # run again…
Robot online. Welcome back, Arun!
        
      
        
      
        
        
        🦴 Limbs (Arduino, C++)
        
        
        robot/firmware/limbs/limbs.ino: moves 6 servos smoothly within safe limits, drives the wheels, reads the distance sensor, and stops the wheels by itself if the brain goes quiet.
        

        S 4 90     right shoulder to 90°
S 5 120    right elbow to 120°
M 180 180  both wheels forward
X          stop wheels
D          → "D 42" (cm to nearest object)
